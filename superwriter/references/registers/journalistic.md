# Journalistic

**Furthest from neutral:** inverted pyramid · every contestable claim attributed to a named source · the paragraph as the unit, one or two sentences each

- **Rhythm:** short, brisk, front-loaded. The first sentence carries the news; each after it adds a diminishing detail. Sentences rarely past 30 words.
- **Syntax:** mostly simple and compound. Active voice, strong verbs, subject early. Attribution appended (*…, the company said*). Names and numbers specific.
- **Distance:** impersonal third person. The reporter is invisible; anything contestable is credited to a source. No first person, no direct address.
- **Selection:** who, what, when, where, then why and how, in that order of priority. Most important fact first even if it happened last. Background near the bottom, cuttable.
- **Emotion:** kept out of the reporter's voice; carried by quotes and facts. Restraint is the convention even on dramatic material.
- **Dialogue:** direct quotes as evidence, one speaker per paragraph, *said* as the tag, attribution after the first clause. Paraphrase between quotes.
- **Paragraph:** one to two sentences. Any paragraph could be the last without breaking the piece.
- **Diction:** plain, concrete, wide-audience. No unglossed jargon. Neutral attribution verbs (*said*, not *claimed* or *admitted*, which editorialize).
- **Figures:** sparse in hard news: one vivid detail rather than a metaphor. A feature lede allows a single image, then back to plain.
- **Unusual:** severable by design: written so an editor can cut from the bottom up and lose nothing essential. Every fact kept is load-bearing enough to attribute.

**Writing it:** lead with the newest, most important fact; attribute anything disputable; keep paragraphs to a sentence or two; cut adjectives. A breathless feature voice with unsourced claims is the caricature.
