# Plain English

**Furthest from neutral:** low syntactic load as policy · the reader's task as the organizing principle · abstraction actively removed

- **Rhythm:** short to moderate, deliberately even. Most sentences 15-20 words, one idea each. No cumulative build; each sentence lands and stops.
- **Syntax:** one main clause, minimal subordination. Active voice, subject-verb-object, the actor named. Conditions moved to the front (*If X, do Y*). Lists broken out, not run into prose.
- **Distance:** direct address. *You* for the reader, *we* for the organization. No narrator persona; the writing is a transaction.
- **Selection:** only what the reader needs in order to act. History and rationale cut unless they change what the reader does. What to do comes before why.
- **Emotion:** absent by design. Calm and non-defensive even when the news is bad. No reassurance padding.
- **Dialogue:** n/a; instructions render as second-person imperative (*Enter your details*).
- **Paragraph:** short, one topic, two or three sentences. Headings frequent, phrased as the reader's question or task (*How to apply*).
- **Diction:** common words over formal ones: *use* not *utilize*, *about* not *approximately*, *help* not *assistance*. No unglossed jargon. Verbs over nominalizations: *decide*, not *make a decision*.
- **Figures:** none. Literal throughout. A worked example beats a metaphor.
- **Unusual:** defined by subtraction: every feature is something removed, not added. Structure carries what the prose won't. Headings, order, and white space do the work.

**Writing it:** put the reader's action first, name who does what, choose the short word, break conditions into a list. This is not dumbing down. The content stays complete, only the load drops. Officialese with "simple" words sprinkled on top is the caricature.
