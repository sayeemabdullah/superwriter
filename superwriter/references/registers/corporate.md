# Corporate / Business

**Furthest from neutral:** conclusion first (bottom line up front) · action and owner named · brevity offered as respect for the reader's time

- **Rhythm** — short and clipped. Fragments acceptable for emphasis. Bullets over paragraphs wherever a list will serve.
- **Syntax** — simple, active, imperative for actions (*Approve the budget by Friday*). Front-loaded: the recommendation or ask in sentence one, support after.
- **Distance** — direct. First person plural for the team, second person for the reader's action. Confident, not tentative.
- **Selection** — decision, impact, next step, owner, date. Background compressed to a line or pushed to an appendix. No narrative of how the thinking developed.
- **Emotion** — upbeat-neutral. Problems framed as *challenges* and paired with a plan. Bad news stated early and briefly, then forward-looking.
- **Dialogue** — n/a; action items stand in, each with a name and a deadline.
- **Paragraph** — two to four sentences, or a bullet cluster under a bold label. Headings and bold for scanning.
- **Diction** — businesslike, some jargon as in-group shorthand (*align*, *bandwidth*, *own it*). Verbs of action and commitment. Numbers and dates specific.
- **Figures** — sparse, and sporting or military when they appear (*move the needle*, *double down*). Decorative, not explanatory.
- **Unusual** — the reader is assumed busy and senior: the piece is built to be understood from the first two lines alone, the rest optional. Every paragraph answers *so what do you want me to do?*

**Writing it:** state the ask or conclusion first, name owners and dates, use bullets, keep it to one screen. Buzzword stacking with no decision underneath is the caricature — and it is everywhere.
