# Legal / Contractual

**Furthest from neutral:** precision over readability by design · defined terms doing the work pronouns would · exhaustive enumeration

- **Rhythm:** long, flat, unvaried. No emphasis and no cadence; rhythm is irrelevant to the function. A sentence may run to a paragraph.
- **Syntax:** heavily hypotactic, conditions and exceptions nested deep. *Provided that*, *notwithstanding*, *subject to*, *for the avoidance of doubt*. Enumerated sub-clauses, (a)(i)(A), keep the nesting parseable.
- **Distance:** impersonal third person. Parties named by defined term (*the Disclosing Party*) rather than pronoun, to remove ambiguity.
- **Selection:** every contingency the drafter can foresee, stated explicitly. Nothing left to inference; if it matters, it is written. Silence is a loophole.
- **Emotion:** none. Adversarial content in wholly neutral form.
- **Dialogue:** n/a.
- **Paragraph:** the numbered clause is the unit. One obligation or definition per clause, cross-referenced by number.
- **Diction:** Latinate; archaic only where the archaism is settled (*herein*, *thereof*, *shall* for obligation). Defined terms capitalized and used identically every time. No elegant variation. The same thing gets the same word.
- **Figures:** none. Metaphor is ambiguity, and ambiguity is a defect.
- **Unusual:** repetition is a virtue: naming a defined term twenty times beats one pronoun that could be misread. Written for a hostile reader hunting a gap, not a cooperative one.

**Writing it:** define terms and reuse them exactly, enumerate every case, prefer *shall* for duties, never vary a word for style. Sprinkling *heretofore* on ordinary prose is the caricature; real drafting closes gaps, it doesn't sound old.
