# Academic

**Furthest from neutral:** claims hedged to their actual strength and attributed · nominalized abstraction · the field foregrounded over the writer

- **Rhythm** — long, even, unhurried. Little variance; the pace signals deliberation. Multiple qualifying clauses carried without a break.
- **Syntax** — densely hypotactic. Subordinate clauses for condition, concession, and citation. Passive where the agent is the field rather than a person (*it has been argued*). Main verb often delayed behind a long subject.
- **Distance** — impersonal. First person confined to signposting (*I argue that*). The writer stands behind the literature; positions are attributed, not asserted bare.
- **Selection** — prior work, method, evidence, limitation. What is included is governed by what a reader could challenge. Nothing stated without support.
- **Emotion** — suppressed. Disagreement rendered as *this account is incomplete*, not *this is wrong*. Stakes framed as contribution and gap.
- **Dialogue** — n/a; reported positions stand in (*Smith contends …; however, …*).
- **Paragraph** — long, one claim developed through evidence and qualification. Topic sentence first, implication last. Explicit connectives between paragraphs.
- **Diction** — Latinate, abstract, discipline-specific. Nominalizations (*the operationalization of*). Hedges throughout: *may*, *appears to*, *tends to*, *arguably*, *to some extent*.
- **Figures** — rare and dead when present (*lens*, *framework*, *landscape*). A fresh metaphor reads as unserious.
- **Unusual** — the epistemic caution *is* the style: every claim carries its confidence level on its face, and sentences are built to survive a hostile reviewer.

**Writing it:** attribute positions, hedge each claim to its real strength, prefer the abstract noun, tie every paragraph to the argument explicitly. Passive voice and long words with no argument underneath is the caricature — and a common one.
