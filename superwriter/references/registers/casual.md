# Casual / Conversational

**Furthest from neutral:** fragments and elision as the default unit, not the exception · shared context assumed and never explained · warmth carried by rhythm and punctuation, not stated

- **Rhythm:** short bursts, then a run-on chained with *and* or *so* when the writer is excited or venting. Punctuation does the pacing: a trailing ellipsis, an exclamation point used sparingly enough to still mean something.
- **Syntax:** fragments as the default, not a device. Subject dropped when obvious (*Heading out now*, not *I am heading out now*). Contractions everywhere, including doubled ones (*wouldn't've*) when that's really how the writer talks.
- **Distance:** direct, first and second person, no persona. Reads as one specific person talking to another, not an institution or a narrator.
- **Selection:** only what this particular reader needs. Shared context assumed and left unexplained (*you know the one*). Callbacks stand in for recap; backstory gets dropped.
- **Emotion:** stated plainly and in the moment (*ugh*, *so annoyed*, *this is great*), no hedging around feelings, no professional distance from them.
- **Dialogue:** reported speech folded in loosely, present tense for effect (*and she's like, no way*), not set off with formal tags.
- **Paragraph:** one to three sentences, often one. Breaks fall wherever the thought turns, not by topic discipline.
- **Diction:** everyday words, slang used the way the writer actually uses it, not stacked on for effect. Texting shorthand only where it's genuinely how the person writes, never as costume.
- **Figures:** occasional, drawn from ordinary daily life, never elaborate or sustained.
- **Unusual:** it reads exactly the way this person messages a friend, not a performance of "casual" layered over formal structure. Restraint is what separates it from parody.

**Writing it:** let the sentence stop where the thought does, drop the subject when it's obvious, keep the contractions, and let punctuation carry the feeling instead of adjectives. Slang and emoji stacked on every line, or a chatty tone bolted onto otherwise formal sentence structure, is the caricature failure.
