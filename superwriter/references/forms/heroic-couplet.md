# Heroic Couplet

**Furthest from neutral:** the closed couplet as a complete unit of sense · balance and antithesis inside the pair · wit built from the rhyme, not decorated by it

- **Meter** — iambic pentameter, tightly kept. Regularity is part of the poise; a caesura near the middle sets the two halves up for balancing.
- **Lineation** — the line is a strong unit and the couplet a stronger one. Each line often splits at the caesura into balanced halves.
- **Rhyme** — full, on adjacent lines: AABBCC. Each pair closes with a click. Slant rhyme dilutes the form's snap.
- **Stanza** — the couplet itself. Verse paragraphs are built by stacking couplets, but each pair should stand alone if lifted out.
- **Enjambment vs end-stop** — strongly end-stopped. The couplet is "closed" — its sense completes at the second rhyme. Running sense past the couplet is rare and pointed; do it habitually and the form is gone.
- **Turn** — a small turn inside almost every couplet: the second line completes, qualifies, or reverses the first, and the rhyme word often delivers the point.
- **Sound** — controlled. Alliteration used to balance halves, not for texture.
- **Compression** — high within the pair — a couplet is an epigram's worth of room. Antithesis (this, not that) does the compressing.
- **Selection** — satire, argument, portrait, maxim. Content that resists being cut into pairs fights the form.
- **Figurative habit** — pointed and brief: a simile filling half a line, not an extended image.

**Writing it:** think in pairs. Make each couplet complete its own thought and close on its rhyme; balance the two lines, or the two halves of a line, against each other; let the rhyme word carry the wit. Enjambing every couplet, or padding the second line to reach the rhyme, throws away everything the form offers.
