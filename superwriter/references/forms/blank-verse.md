# Blank Verse

**Furthest from neutral:** unrhymed iambic pentameter held as a felt pulse · the verse paragraph, not the stanza, as the unit · speech rhythm pulling against the meter

- **Meter:** iambic pentameter, unrhymed. The pulse must stay audible under the sentence; lose it and the writing is line-broken prose. Substitution is frequent and expressive (trochees, spondees, an extra unstressed syllable) but the ear should still count to ten.
- **Lineation:** line and sentence rarely coincide. Sentences begin and end mid-line; the break works against the grammar so the verse never settles into couplets.
- **Rhyme:** none. Break up accidental rhyme; a closing couplet is borrowed from another form and lands as one.
- **Stanza:** none. The verse paragraph, a block of lines held by one movement of thought, is the unit. Breaks fall where the thought shifts.
- **Enjambment vs end-stop:** enjambment-dominant. The run-on line is the engine: it carries the voice forward and drops weight on the first word after the break. Long end-stopped stretches stiffen it.
- **Turn:** no fixed turn; pivots happen where the argument or the speaker turns, anywhere in the paragraph.
- **Sound:** moderate; assonance and consonance bind lines the absent rhyme would have. Not Anglo-Saxon density.
- **Compression:** low to moderate. There is room; the pressure is rhythmic, not spatial. Inversion for the meter is unnecessary and shows.
- **Selection:** flexible: narrative, dramatic, meditative. The form is a vehicle, not a constraint on content.
- **Diction:** whatever the speaker would use; blank verse spans the plain and the grand, and the meter does the elevating.

**Writing it:** write the sentences first, then set them against the pentameter so grammar and line pull in different directions. Keep the pulse countable and let enjambment carry momentum. Line-broken prose with no metrical spine, or a drift into rhymed couplets, is the caricature.
