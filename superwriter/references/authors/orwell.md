# Orwell

**Furthest from neutral:** plainness pursued as an ethical position · concrete detail deployed as argument

- **Rhythm** — even, unhurried. Moderate length, varied enough to avoid monotony, never showy. The rhythm of someone thinking clearly and in order.
- **Syntax** — mostly simple and compound, subordination where logic requires and not otherwise. Main verb early, active voice by strong preference. Transparent — you never notice it.
- **Distance** — first-person essayistic, or close third observing without commenting on itself. The narrator is present, honest about his own discomfort, unwilling to make himself look better than he was.
- **Selection** — physical particulars carrying the argument: what a room smelled like, what a job cost the body, what a person's hands looked like. Concrete detail is evidence, not colour.
- **Emotion** — stated flatly, once, without amplification. Understatement facing horror. The refusal to raise the voice gives the plain statement its force.
- **Dialogue** — functional and infrequent; plain and sparing in fiction.
- **Paragraph** — unified, argumentative, building to a point. Traditional shape executed cleanly.
- **Diction** — Anglo-Saxon by preference, short words over long, no jargon. Abstractions distrusted and used only when unavoidable. Adjectives earn their place.
- **Figures** — sparse but memorable. Concrete and domestic, never ornamental. A metaphor appears when the argument needs one.
- **Unusual** — recording his own bad behaviour without excusing it, which purchases the reader's trust for everything else. Compressing a political argument into one physical observation.

**Writing it:** choose the short word, cut anything decorative, make the concrete detail carry the argument, admit the uncomfortable thing about yourself. Plainness is not simplicity — the thinking underneath isn't simple.
