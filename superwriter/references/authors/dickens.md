# Dickens

**Furthest from neutral:** animation of the inanimate · accumulative rhythm · comic characterization by fixed tic

- **Rhythm:** long periodic sentences building through repetition and parallel structure toward a landing. Anaphora: the same phrase opening successive clauses. Lists extending past necessity for cumulative effect, then a short sentence to close.
- **Syntax:** elaborately hypotactic in narration, long suspended constructions, present participles opening clauses, inversions for emphasis. Openings pile clause on clause before any main verb.
- **Distance:** omniscient and openly editorializing. Addresses the reader, comments on society, moves between panorama and one interior. Sentiment stated directly without irony's protection.
- **Selection:** everything. Streets, weather, rooms, clothing, faces, objects, institutions. Fog, mud, dust and rain rendered at length and given moral force. A room describes its occupant.
- **Emotion:** stated outright and amplified. Pathos direct, sometimes to excess. Comedy and sentiment sit adjacent without transition.
- **Dialogue:** fixed idiolects; each character has a repeated verbal habit or tic identifying them instantly. Minor characters built almost entirely from one. Mannered and theatrical.
- **Paragraph:** long, accumulating, often built on a repeated opening structure.
- **Diction:** mixed high and low. Latinate formality for satire on institutions, vivid Anglo-Saxon for street life. Neologisms and comic proper nouns.
- **Figures:** very dense and characteristically animating: objects and buildings acquire intention, weather acquires personality, institutions become creatures. Personification is the signature move.
- **Unusual:** the extended atmospheric opening pursuing one condition (fog, mud, wind) through a dozen clauses until it becomes a moral argument about the society beneath it. Characterization economical enough to need only a verbal habit.

**Writing it:** animate the inanimate. Build with anaphora, let lists run long, give every speaker one fixed habit, let sentiment be stated. Long Victorian sentences alone aren't it. Personification and accumulation are the engine.
