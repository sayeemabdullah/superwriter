# Shakespeare

**Furthest from neutral:** syntactic inversion · figurative density · thought compressed into metaphor rather than statement

- **Rhythm:** iambic pulse under even the prose; sense runs past line ends rather than stopping at them. Prose passages (comedy, low characters, madness) looser but still cadenced.
- **Syntax:** heavy inversion: object before verb, verb before subject, displaced modifiers. Subordination stacks but clauses stay speakable in one breath. Main verb withheld late to build pressure. Parenthetical asides interrupt and resume.
- **Distance:** dramatic, no narrator. Interiority via soliloquy where a character reasons aloud and changes his mind mid-speech.
- **Selection:** minimal physical description; the stage supplies it. Argument, feeling, and the movement between them. Almost nothing on rooms, clothing, weather unless load-bearing.
- **Emotion:** stated, then interrogated. Weight sits in the interrogation.
- **Dialogue:** everything. Characters answer each other's images, extending or inverting the previous speaker's metaphor. Wordplay is argument, not decoration; a pun is a move.
- **Diction:** Latinate and Anglo-Saxon deliberately mixed within a line for contrast. Coinages, compounds. Concrete nouns doing abstract work.
- **Figures:** very dense. Metaphors extend, mutate mid-sentence into a second image, pursued past where a modern writer stops. Domains: law, commerce, disease, husbandry, theatre.
- **Unusual:** the mid-speech reversal, a character arriving somewhere he didn't intend by thinking in public. Images mutating as they extend, passing through three domains without losing footing.

**Writing it:** delay resolution. Let characters reason rather than assert. Extend one metaphor further than feels safe. Archaic pronouns optional; inverted architecture is not. *Thou* and *forsooth* over modern syntax is the caricature and is immediately visible.
