# Melville

**Furthest from neutral:** register shifting without warning (technical manual to sermon to soliloquy inside a chapter) · digression as structure

- **Rhythm:** enormously varied. Long rolling periodic sentences with biblical cadence beside plain technical exposition beside dramatic monologue with iambic pulse underneath.
- **Syntax:** hypotactic and expansive. Long suspended constructions, catalogues, apostrophe, direct address to the reader. Inversions borrowed from scripture and the stage.
- **Distance:** unstable by design. A first-person narrator fades for chapters while an omniscient dramatist takes over. Direct address, then technical instruction, then interior soliloquy from a character the narrator couldn't have heard.
- **Selection:** everything, at length: a trade's mechanics rendered exhaustively, natural history, philosophy, the sea. Digressions aren't padding; the accumulated detail builds the world the drama needs.
- **Emotion:** enlarged. Ordinary labour given cosmic significance, stated grandly, sometimes undercut with plain humour.
- **Dialogue:** vernacular sailor speech through to full dramatic monologue. Characters given soliloquies with stage directions embedded in the prose.
- **Paragraph:** long, accumulating, frequently catalogue-shaped. Chapters short and tonally distinct from their neighbours.
- **Diction:** high Latinate and biblical mixed with precise technical vocabulary of a trade. Deliberate archaism. Proper nouns and epithets.
- **Figures:** very dense and cosmological. An object examined until it becomes a symbol, then examined further until the symbolism is questioned. Scripture, myth, sea, craft.
- **Unusual:** tonal whiplash: dry technical classification followed by metaphysical dread with no transition. Refusing to settle a symbol's meaning, examining it from many angles and declining to conclude.

**Writing it:** vary register aggressively between sections. Let a digression run. Render technical detail with real precision. Press an object until it means too much, then question the meaning. Uniformly grand prose isn't it. The shifts are the style.
