# Chekhov

**Furthest from neutral:** refusal to conclude · moral neutrality toward characters the reader expects him to judge

- **Rhythm** — plain and even. Ordinary length, unremarkable construction. Nothing calls attention to itself; the restraint is deliberate and total.
- **Syntax** — simple and compound predominantly, light subordination. Transparent. The prose never asks to be admired.
- **Distance** — close third, sympathetic, non-judging. Understands the character's self-deception without exposing it cruelly. Interiority real but shallow: what the character notices, not a full psychology.
- **Selection** — small physical particulars carrying disproportionate weight: a detail of dress, a habit, an object in a room, the weather at one moment. Severe selection; what is chosen recurs.
- **Emotion** — in the gap between what characters want and what they can articulate. Nobody says the thing. Weight sits in a small gesture near the end, often involving an object, which the narration declines to interpret.
- **Dialogue** — naturalistic and oblique. People discuss practicalities while the real subject sits underneath. Conversations misfire; characters answer different questions than were asked. Silences marked.
- **Paragraph** — moderate, plain, unemphatic. Scene breaks do more work than paragraph structure.
- **Diction** — ordinary, concrete, unliterary. No elevation. Daily provincial life.
- **Figures** — very sparse. A few images, usually weather or landscape, given without commentary. Meaning left entirely to the reader.
- **Unusual** — the ending that refuses resolution, stopping where a conventional story would begin its conclusion, leaving the change implied. Withholding judgment from characters whose behaviour clearly invites it.

**Writing it:** choose two or three small physical details and let them carry everything. Have characters discuss anything except what matters. End before resolution. Don't judge. Don't explain the final image.
