# Form Dimensions

Companion to `craft-dimensions.md`, for verse. A form request reads this file plus one
`references/forms/<name>.md`, not `craft-dimensions.md`. The three prose axes that still
matter for verse are carried at the foot of this file.

A form is a set of positions on these axes. Meeting the syllable count is not writing the
form; the pressure that count puts on syntax and selection is.

| # | Dimension | What to attend to |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Meter** | Base foot and line length; how strictly kept; where substitution is licensed. Metrical *tension*, a natural stress fighting the meter, is where meaning shows. |
| 2 | **Lineation** | Where lines break and why: the line as a unit of sense vs. as a unit of sound. What the eye does at the right margin. |
| 3 | **Rhyme** | Scheme and its exactness: full, slant, none. Whether it marks the form or merely ornaments. Early rhyme closes; delayed rhyme holds open. |
| 4 | **Stanza** | Size, and whether the argument maps to it: one thought per stanza, or thought spilling the break. White space as a rest. |
| 5 | **Enjambment vs end-stop** | The ratio. A run-on line speeds the read and throws weight onto the next line's first word; end-stopping slows and seals. |
| 6 | **Turn** | The pivot the form expects (volta, refrain, couplet sting) and *where* it falls. A form is often defined by the position of its turn. |
| 7 | **Sound** | Alliteration, assonance, consonance; density relative to the form's norm. Sound binding unrhymed lines. |
| 8 | **Compression** | What is cut to fit: articles, connectives, half a comparison. Inversion and ellipsis under metrical pressure: deliberate, not padding. |

## Carried from craft-dimensions.md

- **Selection:** what the poem includes and refuses; verse selects harder than prose.
- **Figurative habit:** frequency and kind; whether the images come from one domain.
- **Diction register:** Latinate vs Anglo-Saxon, abstraction, archaism; adjust this last.

## Using these

Name the two or three axes the form is strict about and write to those; let the rest sit at
the form's ordinary. Maximizing every axis produces the exam answer, not the poem.
