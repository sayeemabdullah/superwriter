# House Style

Applies to every generated or transformed piece, on top of the voice. These are the marks
of machine-made text; removing them is most of what makes output read as human.

## Punctuation

- No em dashes or en dashes. Use a comma, a period, a colon, or parentheses. A dash
  parenthetical becomes a comma pair or its own sentence.
- Semicolons stay rare: at most one every few paragraphs, joining two clauses that belong
  in one breath. Never as a dressed-up comma.
- No decorative ellipses, no doubled punctuation.

## Sentences

- Vary length hard. Put a three-word sentence next to a thirty-word one.
- Vary how sentences open. Not three subjects in a row, not two participial openers
  ("Standing there, ...") back to back.
- Fragments are allowed when they land.
- Break parallelism on purpose. Three matched clauses read as generated; make one a
  different shape, or cut the set to two.

## Diction

- Cut: delve, tapestry, realm, crucial, pivotal, robust, seamless, nuanced, and
  figurative navigate or landscape.
- Cut throat-clearers: it's worth noting, that said, moreover, furthermore, notably,
  in essence.
- Plain word over formal one: use not utilize, help not facilitate.

## Rhetoric

- No "not just X but Y", "isn't merely X, it's Y", "more than X, it's Y". State the thing.
- No rhetorical question as a transition. No "here's the thing", "the truth is", "make no
  mistake", "let's be honest".
- Do not hedge every point. Take the position the piece implies.

## Structure

- No summary paragraph the piece did not need. Stop when the point lands.
- No headings, bold labels, or bullet lists unless the user asked or the form requires them.
- No "In conclusion" or "Overall" closer, no uplifting last line.

## When the voice and the house style conflict

The voice wins on rhythm, syntax, diction register, and figurative habit. The house style
wins on the specific tells above. A period-heavy Hemingway pastiche and a long-sentence
Woolf pastiche both obey this file, and neither needs a dash to do it.
